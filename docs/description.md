# Competition description (v0.8.3)

Short text for the competition description field on Apex.

---

Train an ONNX policy that drives a simulated 2-finger gripper on a 4-DoF (x, y, z, yaw) stage through random compound assembly blueprints on a NIST Assembly Task Board #1-style board (MuJoCo, CPU, 25 Hz). Every episode is a freshly generated Assembly IV graph of many steps (typically about 8 to 28): peg inserts, collars, twist-locks, rework (extract a mis-seated peg), press-gated screws, stack rings on peg tops, and yaw-index keys. Random ordering constraints link the steps. Parts arrive in waves. Mass, friction, gain and latency are stretched per episode and can change between subtasks, so policies must identify the system online. Geometry, tolerances and sensor bias are redrawn every episode. Parts start ungrasped and away from their target holes. The observation is 320 floats (288-d Assembly III layout plus a 32-d blueprint encoding). Absolute key yaw is never published; there is no closeness, detent or retarget cue. Score is the mean over blueprint steps of physically verified end-state credit that survives a perturbation test, times precision — equal weight per step. A completed key keeps credit from its own completion latch and recorded precision while its peg remains seated; the wrist may subsequently move to other key windows. Unfinished, unblocked insert, rework, collar, and stack steps can earn at most 0.36 each: 0.12 for a real grasp/lift, plus 0.06 each for held transport toward the hole, held insertion, near-target alignment, and stable release through the perturbation settle. Unfinished key, twist, and screw steps do not earn these generic insertion milestones. Transport and insertion require their physical predecessors, a closed contacting grasp, and actual part motion; released or falling parts do not qualify. Within the lift component, bounded best-so-far credit recognizes a closed two-finger grasp that raises the part 4–8 mm above its settled height for eight consecutive control frames; sub-mm contact jitter, palm pushes and one-finger contact earn none. Contact, wrist proximity, oscillation, reset-only states and passive poses earn nothing. A stack ring mated in play and left undisturbed retains credit; never-mated, displaced, knocked-off and gripped rings score zero. A perfect 1.0 is effectively unreachable. Cost is bounded by the sim horizon only. The round score is the mean over 64 episodes.
